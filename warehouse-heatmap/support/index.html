Quick Start

  1. In Power BI, open Get more visuals, search for Unclocked 3D Heatmap, and add it to your report.
  2. Resize the visual to provide enough room for the warehouse and controls.
  3. Add a unique location field to Location ID.
  4. Add numeric X and Y coordinate fields. Z is optional; add it to position bins at different heights.
  5. Add the numeric measure you want to analyze to Heat Value.
  6. Optionally add bin dimensions and up to three category-filter fields.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the visual create coordinates automatically?

No. Your Power BI model must provide X and Y coordinates for each location. Z coordinates are optional and position bins at different heights. Optional width, length, and height fields define bin dimensions. If you only have a drawing, request warehouse mapping.

What can I use as the Heat Value?

Any numeric Power BI measure that can be evaluated by warehouse location, such as quantity, value, picks, capacity, dwell time, shortage quantity, or exception count.

Can I filter by aisle or level?

Yes. The visual includes three custom category-filter fields that can represent aisle, level, zone, building, process, product family, or another categorical field.

Does it work with Power BI slicers?

Yes. The visual responds to the report filter context, including slicers and interactions configured by the report author.

Is there a 2D view?

Yes. The 2D mode creates a flat bird's-eye plan. When multiple levels overlap, the highest visible level appears on top. Filter to one level when level-specific analysis is required.

How many bins can it display?

The free plan renders up to 2,500 bins. Professional raises the limit to up to 100,000 bins, while the report author controls the scene limit through the visual's Max displayed bins setting. Actual performance depends on the report and device.

Can I use a warehouse floor plan?

Yes. A floor-plan image can be displayed beneath the bins when it is aligned with the same coordinate bounds used by the warehouse data.

Plans, licenses, and sharing

Is Free the same as the Professional trial?

No. Free supports ongoing use with up to 2,500 rendered bins. Professional raises the limit to up to 100,000 bins and has a first-month-free offer on every tier. Review the subscription and trial terms at checkout. Compare the plans.

Does one Professional license cover the whole team?

Professional is priced per user, not per report or warehouse, with lower per-user prices for 11–50, 51–250, and 251–1,000 users. For more than 1,000 users, contact us for a custom offer. Each person who needs the paid rendering capacity should have an assigned visual license, including report viewers. Buying the visual for an author does not license everyone they share a report with.

How do I assign a purchased license?

The purchaser normally receives a license automatically. The purchaser or the appropriate administrator can assign other seats in the Microsoft 365 admin center under Billing → Licenses. Select the visual and assign the users who need it, then refresh the report or reopen Power BI Desktop. Allow time for Microsoft to recognize a new assignment.

Microsoft's visual purchasing and license-assignment guide

Is this included with Power BI Pro or Premium?

The visual subscription is separate from Power BI licensing. Your organization still needs the Power BI licenses or capacity required for its report-sharing setup. Professional is the name of this visual's paid plan; it is not Power BI Pro or Premium.

What about embedded reports or Report Server?

Where Power BI cannot verify licenses, including Power BI Report Server, Publish to web, app-owns-data embedding, and national clouds, the visual stays at the free 2,500-bin limit. Microsoft's visual license management is not supported in every environment. Review Microsoft's environment limitations before planning an embedded or Report Server deployment, and contact support about your setup.

Troubleshooting

No bins appear

Confirm that Location ID, X, Y, and Heat Value are populated. Check that coordinates and Heat Value are numeric, report filters are not hiding the layout, and Max displayed bins is high enough. If you use Z, check that it contains valid numbers too.

I purchased Professional but still see a limit

Check that the visual license is assigned to the same work or school account used in Power BI. Refresh the report or reopen Desktop after assignment. Also check Max displayed bins: purchasing a license does not remove your report's configured display limit. See license guidance.

Bins overlap or appear in the wrong place

Confirm that coordinates and dimensions use the same measurement units, X/Y values identify the intended starting corner, and stacked locations have distinct Z coordinates.

The model is slow to navigate

Switch to the Performance preset, turn off bin outlines and hover tooltips, lower Max displayed bins, or use report filters to reduce the active layout.
